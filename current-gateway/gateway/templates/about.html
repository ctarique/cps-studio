About CPS Studio

The story, research motivation, and design idea behind the platform.

ORIGIN

Why CPS Studio

Cyber-physical systems sit at the intersection of computing and the physical world. Software, networks, sensors, and devices work together so a digital action can trigger something physical, or a physical event can report itself back into software.

Think of the simplest version: a sensor sending readings to a computer. Now scale that up to a robot or an autonomous vehicle reacting to what's around it, and you start to see why these systems matter.

The project began with a question about hands-on computing education:

How can students meaningfully interact with real cyber-physical systems without requiring unrestricted access to all of the computers, networks, firmware, and devices that make those systems work?

CPS Studio is one attempt at answering that.

ARCHITECTURE

The System

At the center of CPS Studio is a Raspberry Pi Gateway.

Students access the Gateway Portal through a designated Lab Workstation, where they can choose from CPS Apps such as LED Flasher and Autonomous Car.

The Gateway communicates with the ESP32-based CPS Devices over a private Wi-Fi network. Each CPS App runs laboratory-controlled firmware.

The Gateway mediates interactions, and applies the necessary boundaries for access control.

This allows students to interact with real hardware without needing direct access to the Pi, CPS Device firmware, or underlying infrastructure.

PHYSICAL SYSTEMS

From Software to Physical Behavior

There's something satisfying about writing a few lines of code and watching something happen in the real world. It's one of the reasons hands-on experimentation can make learning so much more interesting.

An action on the Gateway Portal is checked before a supported command reaches a CPS Device. The device also reports information back to the Pi.

A CPS App could be a short program that uses a few GPIO controls to turn something on and off, or it could be something more complex like a motor responding to a command, or a sensor reporting changes in its surroundings.

These experiments bring together subjects that are often taught separately: embedded programming, networking, device communication, automation, monitoring, and cybersecurity.

FUTURE WORK

Looking Ahead

CPS Studio was designed with room to grow. As new hardware and firmware are introduced, additional CPS Apps can bring more experiments and capabilities to the laboratory.

There are plenty of possibilities to explore, from simple sensor experiments to more complex robotic systems.

What won't change is the underlying idea: students should have enough access to genuinely learn about cyber-physical systems without needing the keys to everything behind them.

Research context

CPS Studio was developed for graduate research in cyber-physical systems security and hands-on computing education.
